2. Search, inclusion, and documentation

  • Search cutoff: October 16, 2026. Sources include Tobii per-game documentation, Steam listings, author itch.io and GitHub projects, institutional papers and repositories, developer pages, Microsoft Research, SpecialEffect, Smartbox, Sensory Guru, official awards, and university reports.
  • Representative searches included eye tracking game, gaze controlled game, blink game, gaze game research prototype, Steam eye tracking, IGF Close Your, gaze accessibility games, Eyes First, and named titles. Citations and project links were followed to earlier work.
  • Inclusion required primary documentation of gaze control, assistance, blink detection, gaze-responsive behavior, or an explicit game-based gaze-measurement study. Tags, names, generic device support, head tracking, or foveated rendering alone were insufficient.
  • Stable IDs support deduplication. Specific research modifications of retail titles are separate, scoped records. Close Your and its later commercial successor Before Your Eyes remain separate works but are not treated as unrelated design lineages. Suites count once; distinctly named and described mini-games may be separate entries, without double-counting a shared study sample.
  • Taxonomy labels are this catalog’s analytic coding of sources and may reflect multiple purposes; they are not a single author-provided taxonomy. Unreported populations, licenses, user studies, and release years are not inferred. Verification dates record documentary review, not gameplay testing.

3. Two axes: design intent and gaze role

Design intent asks why gaze is used: entertainment, accessibility, education/training, rehabilitation research, assessment, artistic expression, and social interaction. Gaze role asks how eyes enter the system: control, assistance, multimodal input, responsiveness, blinking, or measurement only. These axes are recorded separately.

4. Cross-category synthesis

Commercial and simulation: gaze usually supplements conventional input

Truck, flight, racing, and space simulators use eye/head movement to extend viewing, while action games add aiming, tagging, or menu selection. This is not gaze steering. PC integrations do not establish console support. Examples: Euro Truck Simulator 2, DCS World, Elite Dangerous, Deus Ex: Mankind Divided.

Indie and experimental games: the eyes can become the rule

Small games do more than replace the mouse. Stonesight stacks stones with gaze; SobGob makes gazes compete; Don’t Look and Gazed House build horror around lighting, eye contact, or aversion. SuperVision and Twileyed turn tension between seeing and acting into challenge, rather than simply making control easier. Stonesight · SobGob · SuperVision · Twileyed

Accessibility and training: configurability shapes access

EyeMine’s adapted keyboards, Eye Can Fly’s speed and menu conditions, Look Lab’s simplified display, and GazePlay’s open extension illustrate different forms of customization. Eye-only play, gaze plus switches, fallback controls, and caregiver setup should be distinguished. A learning suite’s product positioning is not evidence of clinical efficacy. Minecraft + EyeMine 2 · Eye Can Fly · Look Lab · GazePlay

Research: mechanics, usability, and clinical claims require different evidence

The three UCSD training mini-games share one uncontrolled pilot. Catch the Fruits reports a small usability study. In Limbitless Journey, gaze handles the interface while EMG drives the wheelchair. Atari-HEAD and the Overcooked research environment measure gaze; they do not establish retail gaze control. UCSD study · Catch the Fruits (HVL research prototype) · Limbitless Journey · Atari-HEAD — Atari research game suite · Overcooked — collaborative gaze research environment

VR: edition-specific verification matters

Synapse uses gaze to select telekinesis targets and controllers to move them. Rez Infinite’s gaze aiming is scoped here to its PS VR2 integration. Switchback has blink-dependent enemy mechanics. Head-directed reticles and performance-only foveated rendering were not automatically included. Synapse · Rez Infinite · The Dark Pictures: Switchback VR

6. Research opportunities and missing evidence

The current sample suggests questions rather than proven field-wide gaps: How can non-programmers configure dwell time, trigger regions, and fallback input? How can shared gaze support multiplayer play without extra burden? How can access-skill practice remain distinct from play itself? How can legacy hardware, builds, and licenses remain reproducible? Sparse artistic and social examples may reflect English-language and HCI-source bias, not an absence of work.

7. Limits, unresolved leads, and reuse

  • There was no complete subscription-database export, independent dual screening, or complete candidate exclusion flow. No fabricated PRISMA screening totals or claim of every game worldwide is made.
  • Related literature and leads such as Shynosaurs, Invisible Eni, and Eye-Riders warrant follow-up; records were not invented when primary gameplay details were insufficient. Technical libraries, head-only input, and mouse-following animation assets were not counted as gaze games.
  • Some publisher pages, old downloads, and regional stores restrict access; records note these limits. Paper year, game release year, and gaze-integration year may differ, so consult sources and notes.
